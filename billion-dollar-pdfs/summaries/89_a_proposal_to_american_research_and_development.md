## Summary

In 1957, Ken Olsen and Harlan Anderson proposed a $70,000 investment from American Research and Development (ARD, led by Georges Doriot — "father of venture capital") for 70% of Digital Equipment Corporation (DEC). This was the first modern venture capital deal. DEC's PDP-1 minicomputer ($120K vs $1M mainframes) democratized computing, enabling interactive use, time-sharing, and the hacker culture at MIT. DEC became the #2 computer company; ARD's $70K returned $355M (5000x). This deal proved the VC model: small equity, high risk, massive upside, active involvement.

## Key Takeaways
- First modern VC deal: ARD + DEC (1957)
- Georges Doriot = father of venture capital
- Minicomputers democratized access (PDP-1: $120K vs $1M)
- Interactive computing → hacker culture → personal computer
- 5000x return validated the VC asset class

## Capital Impact
DEC peak market cap: $14B (1980s). ARD's $70K → $355M. Established VC model: high-risk equity, portfolio approach, active board involvement. Direct lineage to Silicon Valley VC (Doriot taught at HBS; students founded firms).
