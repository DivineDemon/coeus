## Summary

Howard Marks (Oaktree, 2013): 'Sea Change' — the 30-year bond bull market ended. Interest rates bottomed; new regime of rising/volatile rates ahead. Implications: asset prices compress, risk premiums widen, leverage becomes dangerous, income-oriented investing returns. 'The easy money has been made.' Investors must adapt: lower return expectations, higher quality, shorter duration, more selectivity. The 'low return world' requires different behavior.

## Key Takeaways
- 30-year bond bull market ended (2012-2013)
- New regime: rising/volatile rates
- Asset price compression; risk premiums widen
- Leverage dangerous; income investing returns
- Lower return expectations; higher quality; shorter duration
- 'Easy money made'; adapt or underperform

## Capital Impact
Oaktree positioned for rising rates; distressed debt opportunity set expanded. Memo guided institutions through 2013-2022 rate transition. 'Sea Change' is the definitive regime-shift memo.
