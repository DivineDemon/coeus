## Summary

Rich Sutton (2019) argues the biggest lesson from 70 years of AI research: general methods leveraging computation (search, learning) ultimately beat human-knowledge-engineered approaches. Moore's law makes massive computation inevitable; researchers waste time building in human knowledge that plateaus. Examples: chess (deep search beat human knowledge), Go (self-play + search), speech recognition (HMMs/stats beat linguistic rules), computer vision (CNNs beat hand-crafted features). The lesson is bitter because success comes from scaling computation, not human insight.

## Key Takeaways
- Search and learning are the two methods that scale arbitrarily with compute
- Human knowledge helps short-term but plateaus long-term
- Building in 'how we think we think' doesn't work; build meta-methods that discover
- Moore's law guarantees compute will grow; bet on methods that use it
- The contents of minds are irredeemably complex; don't hardcode them

## Capital Impact
Intellectual foundation for the scaling hypothesis and massive compute bets (GPT-3, PaLM, etc.). Justified $100B+ in AI training infrastructure by reframing 'brute force' as the only sustainable strategy.

## Related Mental Models
