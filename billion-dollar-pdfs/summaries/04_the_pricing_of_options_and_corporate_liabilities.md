## Summary

Black & Scholes (1973) derived the Black-Scholes-Merton formula: a closed-form solution for European option pricing by constructing a continuously rebalanced riskless hedge (delta hedging). The key insight: option price is independent of investor risk preferences—it depends only on stock price, strike, time, risk-free rate, and volatility. This completed the theoretical foundation for modern derivatives markets.

## Key Takeaways
- Dynamic hedging eliminates risk → price determined by no-arbitrage, not utility
- Volatility is the only unobservable parameter (implied vol extracted from market prices)

Created the $100T+ global derivatives market. Enabled risk management for institutions, structured products, volatility trading, and modern financial engineering. Nobel Prize 1997 (Scholes & Merton; Black deceased).
