## Summary

Chris Dixon (2010): Disruptive innovations are dismissed as toys because they underperform on established metrics while excelling on new ones. Early microcomputers were 'toys' vs mainframes; early internet was 'toy' vs TV/print; mobile apps were 'toys' vs desktop software. Incumbents ignore them until they improve on the old metrics while dominating new ones. Toys become platforms when they hit critical mass of developers/users.

## Key Takeaways
- Disruption starts in 'toy' territory: worse on old metrics, better on new
- Incumbents rationally ignore (low margin, small market)
- Platform transition: toy → tool → platform
- Developer ecosystem is the leading indicator
- Invest in what smart people do on weekends

## Capital Impact
a16z investment thesis; explained early skepticism of Facebook, Twitter, iPhone, VR, crypto. Dixon's portfolio (Oculus, Coinbase, GitHub) all started as 'toys.'
