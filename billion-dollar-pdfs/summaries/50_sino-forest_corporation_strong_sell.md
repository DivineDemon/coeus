## Summary

George Soros (1987): Financial markets don't reflect reality; they shape it. Reflexivity: cognitive function (participants understand world) + manipulative function (participants change world). Bubbles: trend + misconception reinforce each other. Crash: misconception recognized, trend reverses. 'Markets are constantly in a state of uncertainty and flux.' The 'human uncertainty principle': we cannot know the future because our actions change it.

## Key Takeaways
- Reflexivity: understanding ↔ reality two-way loop
- Bubbles = trend + misconception reinforcing
- Equilibrium is a theoretical fiction
- Fallibility: participants' views are always incomplete
- Reflexive processes are historically contingent
- Regulation needed because markets don't self-correct

## Capital Impact
Soros Fund Management $30B+ peak. Broke Bank of England (1992), Thai baht (1997). Reflexivity theory influenced central bank policy, behavioral finance, macro trading. Open Society Foundations $32B+ given away.
