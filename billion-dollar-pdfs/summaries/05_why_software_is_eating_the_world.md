## Summary

Marc Andreessen (2011) argued that 60 years into the computer revolution, all technology required to transform industries through software finally works at global scale. 2B+ broadband users, smartphones putting full internet in every pocket, cloud computing dropping costs 100x. Software companies are invading every industry: books (Amazon), video (Netflix), music (Spotify), recruiting (LinkedIn), telecom (Skype), cars (Tesla/Google), retail (Wal-Mart logistics), finance (Square/PayPal), healthcare, education, defense.

## Key Takeaways
- Software delivers 100x cost reduction (Loudcloud $150K/mo → AWS $1.5K/mo)
- Every industry becomes software-mediated; incumbents must transform or die
- Winner-take-all dynamics favor Silicon Valley-style startups
- US structural advantages: research universities, risk culture, equity capital, rule of law
- Challenges: economic headwinds, talent shortage, stranded workers, valuation discipline

## Capital Impact
Defined the 2010s venture thesis. Directed >$500B in VC capital to software-enabled disruption across every sector. Justified massive SaaS, marketplace, fintech, and platform valuations.

## Related Mental Models
Creative disruption, zero marginal cost, network effects, platform economics, digital transformation
