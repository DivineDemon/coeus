## Summary

Paul Graham (2013): Early startups should manually recruit users and do unscalable work. Airbnb founders flew to NYC to photograph listings; Stripe founders installed APIs personally; Wufoo sent handwritten notes. Growth comes from effort, not automation. 'Consulting' for early users teaches you the product. Manual work is a feature, not a bug — it's how you learn what to build.

## Key Takeaways
- Manual user acquisition teaches product-market fit
- 'Consulting' = learning; automate only after you know what works
- Founders doing sales/support = direct feedback loop
- Unscalable acts create loyalty (handwritten notes, personal onboarding)
- The 'do things that don't scale' phase is temporary but essential

## Capital Impact
Y Combinator core curriculum; shaped 3000+ startups. Airbnb, Stripe, Dropbox, DoorDash all did this. Counterintuitive but proven: manual → automated is the only path to product-market fit.

## Related Mental Models
Product-market fit, founder-led sales, manual → automated, learning loops, early adopter intimacy
