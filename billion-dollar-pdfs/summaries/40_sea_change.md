## Summary

Howard Marks' 2013 Oaktree Capital memo "Sea Change" declared the end of the 30-year bond bull market. After three decades of falling interest rates providing a massive tailwind for all asset prices, rates had bottomed and a new regime of elevated, volatile rates was beginning. This shifts the investment landscape fundamentally: credit and value strategies become winners; growth and duration become losers. The "easy money" era is over. Investors must adapt with lower return expectations, higher quality focus, shorter duration, and more selectivity. The memo introduced the concept of "regime change" in market cycles.

## Key Takeaways
- 30-year bond bull market ended (2012-2013)
- New regime: rising/volatile rates
- Asset price compression; risk premiums widen
- Leverage dangerous; income investing returns
- Lower return expectations; higher quality; shorter duration
- "Easy money made"; adapt or underperform

## Capital Impact
Oaktree positioned for rising rates; distressed debt opportunity set expanded. Memo guided institutions through 2013-2022 rate transition. "Sea Change" is the definitive regime-shift memo.

## Related Mental Models
