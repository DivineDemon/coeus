## Summary

Bill Gates (1995) internal memo to Microsoft: 'The Internet is the single most important development since the IBM PC.' Urged reorienting every product around the Internet or being swept aside. Predicted: content explosion, audio/video streaming, online commerce, Internet as universal communication medium, Microsoft must lead not follow. Famous quote: 'We have to make sure we're the leaders, not the followers.'

## Key Takeaways
- Internet adoption follows S-curve; inflection point = NOW (1995)
- Every Microsoft product must have Internet integration
- Content and commerce will move online; early movers win
- Browsers become the new OS platform; threaten Windows dominance
- 'Embrace, extend, extinguish' strategy implicitly begins here

## Capital Impact
Triggered Microsoft's Internet pivot (IE, MSN, ActiveX, .NET), secured Windows dominance for 20 more years, catalyzed the dot-com boom ($1T+ value creation), and established Gates as tech visionary.

## Related Mental Models
Platform shifts, inflection points, embrace-extend-extinguish, browser wars, network effects
