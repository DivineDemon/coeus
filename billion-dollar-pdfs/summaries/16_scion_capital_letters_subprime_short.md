## Summary

Michael Burry (2005-2008) letters to investors detailing the 'Big Short' thesis: subprime mortgage-backed securities (MBS) would collapse due to teaser rates resetting, negative amortization, and fraudulent underwriting. Burry bought CDS protection on MBS tranches, facing years of mark-to-market losses before 2008 vindication. Scion returned ~489% net (2000-2008) vs S&P -9%. The letters show rigorous bottom-up analysis of loan-level data.

## Key Takeaways
- Structural flaws in securitization: originators don't hold risk
- Teaser rate resets + negative amortization = payment shock wave
- Rating agencies conflicted; AAA ratings on toxic collateral
- Contrarian investing requires conviction + capital to withstand drawdowns
- CDS on MBS was asymmetric bet: small premium, massive payoff

## Capital Impact
Burry personally made $100M+; fund returned ~$700M for investors. Inspired 'The Big Short' book/film. Validated forensic credit analysis; changed how regulators view structured products.

## Related Mental Models
Asymmetric bets, forensic accounting, contrarian investing, structural credit analysis, CDS mechanics
