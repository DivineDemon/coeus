## Summary

Tim Berners-Lee (1989) proposed a linked hypertext system at CERN to share information without central control. The 'WorldWideWeb' would use hypertext links across a network, allowing any document to reference any other. Key concepts: universal document identifiers (UDIs), simple protocols, client-server model, hypertext markup. This was the founding document of the World Wide Web.

## Key Takeaways
- Decentralized linking > centralized databases for information sharing
- Universal identifiers (URLs) enable global addressability
- Simple text-based protocols (HTTP/HTML) win over complex systems
- Bootstrapped from CERN's need; became universal platform
- 'This proposal is not the final design' — iterative evolution built in

## Capital Impact
Created the Web ($100T+ digital economy). Enabled Google, Amazon, Facebook, e-commerce, social media, cloud computing. Berners-Lee gave it away royalty-free, maximizing adoption.

## Related Mental Models
Decentralized protocols, network effects, open standards, platform bootstrapping, digital commons
