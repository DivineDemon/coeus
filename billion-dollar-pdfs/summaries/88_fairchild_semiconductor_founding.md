## Summary

The 1957 founding of Fairchild Semiconductor by the "Traitorous Eight" (including Gordon Moore, Robert Noyce, Eugene Kleiner) who left Shockley Semiconductor established the template for Silicon Valley. Fairchild invented the planar process (Jean Hoerni), making mass-produced integrated circuits possible. Noyce and Moore later founded Intel; Kleiner co-founded Kleiner Perkins; others founded AMD, National Semiconductor, and venture capital firms. The Fairchild DNA — equity for engineers, meritocracy, spin-out culture — created the world's most powerful innovation ecosystem.

## Key Takeaways
- Shockley's management style drove out top talent (the Traitorous Eight)
- Planar process = manufacturable ICs = semiconductor industry foundation
- Fairchild alumni founded: Intel, AMD, National Semi, Kleiner Perkins, Sequoia Capital
- Equity culture: engineers as partners, not employees
- Spin-out > acquisition: Valley's replication engine

## Capital Impact
Fairchild spawned the entire semiconductor industry ($500B+ market). Intel alone: $200B+ peak. Kleiner Perkins/Sequoia funded Google, Amazon, Apple, Cisco, etc. The "Fairchild tree" maps to >$10T in market cap. Venture capital model codified here.
