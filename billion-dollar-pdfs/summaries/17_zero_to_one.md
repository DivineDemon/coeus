## Summary

Peter Thiel (2012, CS183 notes by Blake Masters): True progress is vertical (0→1) creating new things, not horizontal (1→n) copying. Monopolies (Google, Tesla) capture value; competition destroys it. Secrets exist: important truths few agree with. Startups should find secrets, build monopolies, start small and dominate niche, then scale. Power law: one company outperforms all others combined. Definite optimism: future is knowable and shapeable.

## Key Takeaways
- Monopoly = durable value capture; competition = commodity trap
- Secrets are undiscovered truths; find them in nature or people
- Start small, dominate niche, then expand (Amazon: books → everything)\- Power law distribution: portfolio returns driven by single outlier
- Definite optimism > indefinite optimism/pessimism

## Capital Impact
Defined modern VC thesis (Founders Fund, a16z). Thiel's investments: Facebook, Palantir, SpaceX, Airbnb, Stripe. Book sold 2M+ copies; required reading in Silicon Valley.

## Related Mental Models
Power law, monopoly theory, secrets, definite optimism, vertical progress
